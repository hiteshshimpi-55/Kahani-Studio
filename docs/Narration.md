# Types of Narration: Literature & Audio Content

## Part 1: Narration in Literature (Point of View)

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **First Person** | Narrator is a character in the story ("I," "me," "we"). Intimate but limited to that character's knowledge. | *The Catcher in the Rye* |
| **Second Person** | Narrator addresses the reader directly as "you." Rare in fiction, common in nonfiction/interactive fiction. | *Bright Lights, Big City*; Choose Your Own Adventure books |
| **Third Person Omniscient** | All-knowing narrator; can access any character's thoughts and information unknown to characters. | *Pride and Prejudice*, *1984*, *Harry Potter* |
| **Third Person Limited** | Narrator is outside the story but sticks closely to one character's thoughts/knowledge. | Hemingway's short stories |
| **Third Person Objective** | Narrator reports only observable actions/dialogue — no access to any inner thoughts (like a camera). | — |
| **Unreliable Narrator** | Narrator's credibility is compromised, prompting readers to question the account. | — |
| **Multiple/Alternating Narrators** | Story alternates between different characters' viewpoints, often chapter by chapter. | — |
| **Free Indirect Discourse (FID)** | Blends narrator's third-person voice with a character's inner voice, dropping tags like "he thought." | Modern literary fiction |

## Part 2: Narration in Audio Content (General Industry)

### By Narrator Count
- **Solo Narration** — One voice actor narrates the entire book/piece, regardless of how many characters exist.
- **Multicast / Duet Narration** — Two or more voice actors, each playing a different character; essentially an audio play. Often called a "Dramatization." Best suited to plays, children's books, fantasy.

### By Voicing Style (within Solo Narration)
- **Fully-Voiced** — Every character gets a distinguishable voice via the narrator's skill with intonation, accent, and mannerism.
- **Partially-Voiced** — Only key characters (e.g., protagonist) get distinct voicing; others stay close to the narrator's neutral tone.
- **Unvoiced** — Flat, neutral read with no character differentiation.

### By Content/Industry Category
- **Audiobook Narration** — Requires character voicing, emotional delivery, and consistency across a long work.
- **Documentary Narration** — Clear, authoritative voice; adapts tone from serious historical content to lighthearted nature shows.
- **Podcast Narration** — Conversational, informal, varied voices; prioritizes casual discussion over structured narrative.
- **Traditional Audioplays / Radio Drama** — Multiple actors + sound effects/soundscapes create an immersive dramatized world without visuals.
- **E-learning / Corporate / Commercial / Automotive Narration** — Tone, pace, and expression tailored to purpose (instructional, trustworthy, persuasive, technical).
- **ADR / Dubbing** — Replacing original dialogue with a new voice, often for foreign-language localization.
- **Audio Description** — Descriptive narration of visual content for visually impaired audiences.
- **AI/Synthetic Narration** — TTS and voice-cloning technology generating narration without a human voice actor (e.g., Sogou's AI reading avatars of Chinese authors).

## Part 3: Narration Styles on Pocket FM–Type Platforms

Platforms like Pocket FM, Kuku FM, and similar serialized audio-drama apps blend several styles together rather than using just one:

1. **Dramatized/Voiced Narration with a Single Narrator**
   One main narrator voice handles description/exposition, shifting tone for each character's dialogue. Increasingly produced via **AI text-to-speech (TTS)** rather than human voice actors, cutting cost and production time.

2. **Multicast/Ensemble Voicing** (for premium/flagship titles)
   Multiple distinct voice actors per character, layered with high-end sound design — used for major adaptations (e.g., Pocket FM's *Solo Leveling* audio series) to rival cinematic, visual storytelling.

3. **Soundscape-Layered Narration** (the signature style)
   Narration/dialogue layered over background music, ambient effects, and audio cues (footsteps, weather, doors, etc.) to build an immersive world — closer to "cinematic audio drama" than a plain audiobook read.

4. **Episodic/Serialized Narration**
   Content is broken into short, cliffhanger-driven episodes structured for daily bingeing (like TV episodes) rather than one continuous narration.

**Note on quality variance:** Since much of this is AI-generated, quality can vary — some listeners report AI narrators sounding synthetic, with male AI voices sometimes failing to clearly differentiate between male and female characters.
